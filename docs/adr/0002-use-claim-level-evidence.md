# Use claim-level evidence for material administrative assertions

Evidence is attached to semantic administrative claims, not to every sentence or piece of presentation copy. A published claim must carry an Evidence Link to a preserved Source when it materially tells a person what the government requires, permits, charges, accepts, how long something takes, which dependency applies, or where they may act. This includes consequential Procedure-selection and eligibility conditions, document requirements and quantities, fees, validity or turnaround claims, material procedural steps, dependencies, routing/jurisdiction conditions, and Practical Preparation presented as Field Guidance.

Derived explanations, Question wording, Goal and Procedure labels, UI grouping, deterministic personalization text, product safety warnings, and summaries that introduce no new administrative assertion do not require their own Evidence Links. They may inherit the provenance of the underlying claims they present.

Evidence Links record the exact relied-upon passage and retrieval/applicability context. Procedure-level bibliographies remain insufficient for evidence-bearing claims because they cannot show which source supports which assertion or isolate stale and disputed guidance.

Persistence and presentation are separate concerns. The system retains granular claim-level provenance internally, while the public interface may group sources, show compact verification metadata, or disclose claim-specific evidence on demand instead of rendering a citation beside every sentence. This preserves auditability and user trust without forcing the storage model to dictate the visual design.
